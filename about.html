Artist statement

Artist Name

I was born and raised in Istanbul, Türkiye, and have a background in fashion and textile design, which influences how I now make innovative, spontaneous, and critical paintings. I carry forward fashion and textiles' use of sampling and appropriation, using specific designs, textures, and color combinations. I also reference past color field painting and painters who bring process forward as an explicit carrier of meaning. I'm interested in how legacies, contexts, and meaning in painting can change, and how painterly vocabularies and meanings can be reclaimed and challenged.

I want my paintings to be joyful, euphoric, inquisitive, and aware of their process and history. The way I make the work is initially experimental and research-based, often beginning with preliminary digital studies such as exploring color and material combinations.

I use an absorbent wet ground that I work directly into, making marks that spread, bind, and move together, creating a rhythm and a controlled, accidental spread. A different mark then appears: a honeycomb, bubble wrap, dot motif; it is present at different levels in every painting I make. It repeats in varying ways, adjusting its arrangement to each painting's needs.

The bubble wrap motif is used simultaneously in two ways. First, it functions like any other pictorial device or series of marks; it allows multiplicity and a reflexive, post-Impressionistic, euphoric, optimistic energy into the paintings. A second reading is that the bubble wrap is also a sign of abstraction's fate; it will be largely wrapped and unseen in today's saturated image-soaked world. Although applied for pictorial unity and beauty, it also functions as a mechanical sign of plastic packaging, asphyxiation, and the doom world of late industrial capitalism.
